# FeralOS — The Meaning of the Name

## Etymology

**FeralOS** comes from the English term **"feral"**, which in turn derives from the Latin **"feralis"** (belonging to the dead, ominous) and earlier from **"ferus"** (wild, fierce).

In modern English, **"feral"** means:
- **Wild** — living in a free state, untamed
- **Untamed** — not having been domesticated, retaining its own nature
- **Feral** — roaming free, not controlled by humans

## The Connection with Alpine Linux

The choice of this name is no coincidence. FeralOS is a **fork of Alpine Linux**, a distribution born in the **Alps** — Europe's most iconic mountains. The name creates a perfect semantic bridge:

### The Mountain as Metaphor

The Alps represent:
- **Solidity** — ancient, immovable rock that endures for centuries
- **Freedom** — open peaks, infinite sky, absence of boundaries
- **Wildness** — pristine forests, wild fauna, intact ecosystem
- **Resilience** — ability to survive in extreme conditions

FeralOS embodies these qualities in the world of software:
- **Solidity** → Memory-safe architecture, Rust and Go components
- **Freedom** → Open source, transparent code, no hidden dependencies
- **Wildness** → Not conforming to conventions, seeking better solutions
- **Resilience** → System that resists attacks, recovers from crashes

### The Bear as Spirit

In the Alps lives the **brown bear** (*Ursus arctos*) — from Bern's heraldry to the Trentino forests, the most charged symbol of Alpine wilderness. Extirpated from the mountains, then reintroduced (the *Life Ursus* project): the Alps are rewilding, and the bear is the wild coming back.
- **Feral** in the purest sense — wild, untamed, never domesticated
- **Powerful** — strength without excess, every action has a purpose
- **Resilient** — survives the long winter and the harshest climates
- **Self-sufficient** — autonomous, territorial, needs no permission to exist

FeralOS aims to be the bear of the Linux world:
- **Powerful** — memory-safe core, built to last
- **Resilient** — resists attacks, recovers from crashes
- **Self-sufficient** — Alpine as base, minimal overhead, no useless components
- **Rewilding** — like the bear returning to the Alps, FeralOS brings the wild back to Alpine

## The "Feral" Philosophy

### 1. Untamed

Most Linux distributions are "tamed" — they follow market rules, compromise to meet business needs, accept compromises for compatibility.

FeralOS is **feral**:
- **Rejects C when memory-safe alternatives exist** — uutils instead of coreutils, micro instead of vim
- **Does not compromise on security** — doas instead of sudo, zero unnecessary components
- **Seeks excellence** — not the "good enough"

### 2. Wild but Structured

Being "feral" does not mean being chaotic. On the contrary:
- **Rigid architecture** — standard core, pluggable data
- **Clear rules** — configurations are declarative, not imperative
- **Clean code** — Go for the installer, Rust for system components

Wild is the **philosophy**, not the **method**.

### 3. Untamed in Vision

FeralOS does not stop at the installer:
- **Phase 1** → Solid and tested installer
- **Phase 2** → Dinit instead of OpenRC, custom repository
- **Phase 3** → Complete distribution, desktop and server

Each phase is a step toward a larger goal: **redesigning how an operating system is born**.

## The Subtitle

> **FeralOS** — *Alpine Linux, Wild*

This subtitle captures the essence:
- **Alpine Linux** — the solid and lightweight base
- **Wild** — the transformation FeralOS brings

FeralOS **is a fork of Alpine** — and the name says exactly that: a *feral* animal is not a new species invented from nothing, it is the **descendant of something established, living free in the wild**. That is what a fork is to its upstream — and it is what FeralOS is to Alpine: **unleashed**, free from obsolete C components, free from security compromises, free to become what Alpine should be.

## Comparison with Other Distributions

| Distribution | Meaning | Philosophy |
|---------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Ubuntu** | "Humanity towards others" | Community, accessibility |
| **Fedora** | Fedora hat | Innovation, cutting edge |
| **Alpine** | The Alps | Minimalism, simplicity |

FeralOS does not compete with these distributions — it **complements** the landscape by offering what is missing: a truly secure distribution, **forked from Alpine**, with a clear and ambitious vision.

## The Name as a Promise

Being called **FeralOS** is a promise:
- **Promise of security** — memory-safe components, zero compromises
- **Promise of freedom** — open source, no hidden dependencies
- **Promise of excellence** — never settling, always improving
- **Promise of wildness** — not following the masses, finding its own path

It is a name that **inspires** and **intimidates** at the same time:
- **Inspiration** — to build something different, something better
- **Intimidation** — for those who think the status quo is sufficient

## Conclusion

**FeralOS** is not just a name. It is a **statement of intent**.

Choosing "feral" means choosing **freedom** over **convenience**, **security** over **compatibility**, **excellence** over **good enough**.

It is the perfect name for a distribution that aims to be:
- **Wild** in principle
- **Solid** in implementation
- **Free** in philosophy

*FeralOS — Alpine Linux, Wild.*
