# FeralOS — The Meaning of the Name

## Etymology

**FeralOS** derives from the English term **"feral"**, which in turn comes from the Latin **"feralis"** (belonging to the dead, deadly) and even earlier from **"ferus"** (wild, fierce).

In modern English, **"feral"** means:
- **Wild** — lives free, not domesticated
- **Untamed** — refused to be tamed, keeps its own nature
- **Feral** — roaming free, not controlled by humans

## The Connection with Alpine Linux

The name choice is not random. FeralOS is based on **Alpine Linux**, a distribution born in the **Alps** — Europe's most iconic mountains. The name creates a perfect semantic bridge:

### The Mountain as Metaphor

The Alps represent:
- **Solidity** — ancient, immovable rock that endures for centuries
- **Freedom** — open peaks, infinite sky, absence of boundaries
- **Wildness** — pristine forests, wild fauna, intact ecosystem
- **Resilience** — ability to survive extreme conditions

FeralOS embodies these qualities in the software world:
- **Solidity** → Memory-safe architecture, Rust and Go components
- **Freedom** → Open source, transparent code, no hidden dependency
- **Wildness** → Do not bend to conventions, look for better solutions
- **Resilience** → System that resists attacks, recovers from crashes

### The Wolf as Spirit

In the Alps lives the **wolf** (*Canis lupus*), the quintessential predator:
- **Feral** in the purest sense — wild, untamed
- **Intelligent** — hunts in packs, strategy, adaptability
- **Efficient** — no waste, every movement has a purpose
- **Necessary** — balances the ecosystem, eliminates the weak

FeralOS aims to be the wolf of the Linux world:
- **Efficient** — Alpine as base, minimal overhead
- **Intelligent** — dynamic rules, automatic configuration
- **Essential** — fills the gap for secure and modern distributions
- **Predatory** — never settles, always seeks better

## The "Feral" Philosophy

### 1. Untamed

Most Linux distributions are "tamed" — they follow market rules, bend to business needs, accept compromises for compatibility.

FeralOS is **feral**:
- **Rejects C when memory-safe alternatives exist** — uutils instead of coreutils, micro instead of vim
- **Does not compromise on security** — doas instead of sudo, zero unnecessary components
- **Seek excellence** — not the "good enough"

### 2. Wild but Structured

Being "feral" does not mean chaos. On the contrary:
- **Rigid architecture** — standard core, pluggable data
- **Clear rules** — configurations are declarative, not imperative
- **Clean code** — Go for the installer, Rust for system components

The wild is the **philosophy**, not the **method**.

### 3. Indomitable in Vision

FeralOS does not stop at the installer:
- **Phase 1** → Solid, tested installer
- **Phase 2** → Dinit instead of OpenRC, custom repository
- **Phase 3** → Full distribution, desktop and server

Every phase is a step toward a bigger goal: **redesigning how an operating system is born**.

## The Subtitle

> **FeralOS** — *Alpine Linux, Wild*

This subtitle captures the essence:
- **Alpine Linux** — the solid and lightweight base
- **Wild** — the transformation FeralOS brings

Not an Alpine fork. It is Alpine **freed** — free from obsolete C components, free from security compromises, free to become what it should be.

| Distribution | Meaning | Philosophy |

## The Name as a Promise

Being named **FeralOS** is a promise:
- **Promise of security** — memory-safe components, zero compromises
- **Promise of freedom** — open source, no hidden dependency
- **Promise of excellence** — never settle, always improve

It is a name that **inspires** and **intimidates** at the same time:

## Conclusion

**FeralOS** is not just a name. It is a **statement of intent**.

Choosing "feral" means choosing **freedom** over **convenience**, **security** over **compatibility**, **excellence** over **good enough**.

It is the perfect name for a distribution that aims to be:
- **Wild** in principle
- **Solid** in implementation
- **Free** in philosophy

*FeralOS — Alpine Linux, Wild.*
